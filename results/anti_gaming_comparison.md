# Q9: Anti-Gaming — metrics with vs. without a serving-time-unavailable feature

Full test splits. Generated by `scripts/run_anti_gaming.py --dataset {ebnerd,mind}`. Raw JSON
(with full bootstrap 95% CIs) at `results/{ebnerd,mind}/anti_gaming.json`.

Our actual retrieval methods (Q2/Q3) never use a feature unavailable at serving time -- the
query is built only from a user's *past* clicks (`recent_history_asof`, cutoff at the
impression's own timestamp), and candidates are scored on static title/abstract text. So there
was no naturally-occurring leaky variant of the pipeline to compare against. This ablation
builds one deliberately: blends BM25's score with `log1p(article popularity)`, computed two
ways -- **safe** (click counts from the TRAIN split only, which entirely precedes val/test in
our `temporal_split`) vs. **LEAKY** (click counts from train+val+test combined -- includes
clicks from the very period being evaluated, which would not be knowable at real serving time).

## EB-NeRD (71,631 test impressions)

| Metric | BM25 alone | +safe popularity | +LEAKY popularity |
|---|---|---|---|
| AUC | 0.4970 | 0.4694 | **0.5426** |
| MRR | 0.3189 | 0.2988 | **0.3435** |
| nDCG@5 | 0.3481 | 0.3238 | **0.3799** |
| nDCG@10 | 0.4322 | 0.4134 | **0.4572** |

## MIND (34,519 test impressions)

| Metric | BM25 alone | +safe popularity | +LEAKY popularity |
|---|---|---|---|
| AUC | 0.5449 | 0.5534 | **0.5644** |
| MRR | 0.2818 | 0.2880 | **0.2917** |
| nDCG@5 | 0.2571 | 0.2652 | **0.2669** |
| nDCG@10 | 0.3184 | 0.3248 | **0.3290** |

## Reading

**The leaky variant beats the safe variant on every metric, on both datasets, with
non-overlapping 95% CIs** -- this is the metric-inflation risk Q9 asks to demonstrate, shown
with real numbers rather than just asserted. Concretely: if you accidentally computed article
popularity over your full dataset (including the test period) instead of strictly
train-before-test, your offline evaluation would report a rosier picture than the system will
actually achieve in production, where those future clicks don't exist yet.

The *size* of the inflation differs a lot by dataset, and it's explainable from the data itself,
not a fluke. EB-NeRD's popularity distribution shifts hugely between train-only and
train+val+test (median clicked-article count 17 -> 74 -- val+test carries a lot of additional
click volume relative to train alone), so the leaked signal is large and the AUC gap is large
(0.469 -> 0.543, +0.073). MIND's shift is much smaller (median 2 -> 3), so there's proportionally
less extra signal to leak, and the gap is correspondingly smaller (0.553 -> 0.564, +0.011) --
still real and statistically clear, just less dramatic. This is itself a useful finding: the
*severity* of a given leakage bug depends on the underlying data distribution, not just on the
bug's existence.

One more finding, secondary to the main point: on EB-NeRD, the *safe* popularity feature
actually **underperforms** pure BM25 (0.497 -> 0.469) -- a naive, unweighted popularity blend
isn't automatically helpful even when it's leak-free, since it can drown out the more
personalized BM25 signal for a global "everyone clicks this" prior. On MIND, safe popularity
mildly helps (0.545 -> 0.553). Worth noting as a reminder that "not leaky" and "good feature"
are two different questions.
