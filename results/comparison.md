# Q2 vs Q3: BM25 vs. embedding-based candidate retrieval

Full test-split runs, `n_recent=10` (query/user embedding built from each user's 10 most
recent clicks, strictly before the impression's own timestamp -- see `feature_store.
recent_history_asof`). Raw JSON per run in this directory; generated by
`scripts/compare_retrieval.py --dataset {ebnerd,mind} --variant "XLM-R=embeddings_eval" --variant "mpnet=embeddings_eval_mpnet"`.

Two embedding variants were run, deliberately: `xlm-roberta-base` (vanilla pretrained, no
retrieval/similarity fine-tuning -- what the assignment names directly) and
`sentence-transformers/paraphrase-multilingual-mpnet-base-v2` (same XLM-R-base architecture
under the hood, but fine-tuned via multilingual knowledge distillation from an English
paraphrase-similarity teacher -- still "computing our own embedding via XLM-RoBERTa", just a
checkpoint that's actually been trained for the job).

## EB-NeRD (71,631 test impressions, 20,738 articles, Danish)

| K | BM25 | XLM-R (raw) | mpnet (fine-tuned) | winner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50  | 0.49% | 0.26% | 0.48% | BM25 |
| 100 | 1.03% | 0.48% | 0.97% | BM25 |
| 200 | 2.01% | 0.95% | 1.69% | BM25 |

Cold-start rate: 0% all three methods.

## MIND (34,519 test impressions, 65,238 articles, English)

| K | BM25 | XLM-R (raw) | mpnet (fine-tuned) | winner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50  | 0.45% | 0.09% | 0.69% | **mpnet** |
| 100 | 0.82% | 0.17% | 1.25% | **mpnet** |
| 200 | 1.43% | 0.37% | 2.27% | **mpnet** |

Cold-start rate: 3.0% all three methods (1,052 / 34,519 -- same population for all three, since
all derive their query/user-vector from the same `recent_history_asof` call).

## Reading

Three findings here, in increasing order of interest:

1. **Fine-tuning matters enormously.** Going from raw `xlm-roberta-base` to the fine-tuned
   `mpnet` checkpoint roughly doubles recall@200 on both datasets (EB-NeRD: 0.95% -> 1.69%;
   MIND: 0.37% -> 2.27%). Vanilla pretrained transformer embeddings, mean-pooled with no
   similarity-specific training, are a known-weak retrieval baseline -- this is exactly why
   Sentence-BERT-style fine-tuning exists, and the gap here is direct empirical confirmation of
   that on our own data.

2. **BM25 still wins on EB-NeRD (Danish), by ~1.2x over the fine-tuned model.** Danish news
   titles/abstracts are information-dense with proper nouns and specific named entities
   (people, places, teams) -- exactly what exact-token lexical matching is good at, and Danish
   morphology (compounding, inflection) doesn't obviously hurt simple regex tokenization here.

3. **mpnet wins on MIND (English), by ~1.6x over BM25.** The likely explanation: `paraphrase-
   multilingual-mpnet-base-v2` is distilled *from* an English-only teacher
   (`paraphrase-mpnet-base-v2`) and then extended to ~50 languages -- so its English semantic
   quality is closer to native, while Danish is a distilled/extended capability. That would
   explain exactly this pattern: strong enough in English to beat lexical matching outright, not
   quite strong enough in Danish to catch up (though it still closes most of the raw-XLM-R gap
   there too).

This is a genuine dataset-dependent result, not an artifact -- and it's a good illustration of
why "semantic should just be better" isn't automatically true: it depends on (a) whether the
embedding model was actually fine-tuned for retrieval/similarity, and (b) whether that
fine-tuning covered the target language well. Absolute recall is low across the board (title-
history-based candidate generation against a 20K-65K article catalog is a genuinely hard
problem at this scale), but the relative comparison -- and its reversal across languages -- is
the headline result for Q3.5 / the design note (Q6).
